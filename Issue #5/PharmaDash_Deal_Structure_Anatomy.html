A "$6 billion deal" is mostly a promise

The headline numbers on these licensing deals are eye-watering — but most of the money is contingent "biobucks" that only get paid if the drug succeeds. Here's how one deal actually breaks down.

Anatomy of the headline number
Worked example: Pfizer → 3SBio, ex-China rights to the PD-1/VEGF bispecific SSGJ-707 (2025)
Plus — not in the headline
Royalties
Tiered double-digit royalties (typically 10–15%) on ex-China sales, paid over years if the drug reaches market. A long-tail stream, separate from the $6.15B figure.

Why this matters

  • The headline is mostly milestones. That $4.8B only gets paid if the drug clears development, regulatory and sales hurdles — and most milestone-laden deals never pay out in full.
  • Upfront is the real conviction signal. Pfizer's $1.25B upfront was a record for a Chinese asset — that committed cash, not the biobucks, is the truest measure of how much a buyer believes.
  • These deals favor the Western buyer. The Chinese partner keeps just 10–15% of ex-China sales — less than a third of the profit — which is part of why restricting them may hurt US pharma more than China.
  • Read "biobucks" with a pinch of salt. A "$6B deal" is a headline, not a cheque. Track the upfront to see what's really being spent.

The industry-wide tell

~6%

Across all biopharma licensing announced in Q1 2026 — roughly $77B in headline value — only about 6% was actually paid upfront in cash. The rest is promise. This is how the whole licensing market works, not just China deals.
