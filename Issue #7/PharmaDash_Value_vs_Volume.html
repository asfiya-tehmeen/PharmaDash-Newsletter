Where the money is vs. where the patients are

The United States buys about half the world's medicine by value — but takes only about a fifth of its doses. That single inversion explains almost everything about how global pharma behaves.

Why this chart is US vs. rest of world

Region-by-region volume shares aren't published in comparable public sources — only spending shares are. Rather than estimate a six-region split we can't verify, this shows the one inversion that is well documented. The ~20% US volume figure is an estimate cited across industry analyses, not an audited statistic.

What the inversion means commercially

  • Price, not usage, drives the gap. Americans don't take dramatically more medicine — they pay far more per dose. US prices are set by market competition; most other countries regulate them.
  • Volume growth ≠ revenue growth. A company can double patients treated in Indonesia and barely move its revenue line. A single US formulary win can change a quarter.
  • The US subsidises global R&D. Roughly half the world's drug spending funds the pipeline everyone else eventually uses — which is precisely what MFN pricing policy is designed to change.
  • Emerging markets are the long game. They own the dose growth. The open question is whether value per patient ever converges toward volume.
