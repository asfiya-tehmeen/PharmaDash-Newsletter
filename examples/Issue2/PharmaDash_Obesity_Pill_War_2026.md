# Pills, Power, and a New Drug King: Inside Pharma's 2026 Obesity War

*How a once-daily pill turned the Lilly - Novo rivalry into the most consequential commercial battle in the industry, and what the Q1 2026 numbers reveal about who's winning.*

---

For the first time since 2023, the world's best-selling drug is no longer Merck's cancer immunotherapy Keytruda. In Q1 2026, that title went to **Mounjaro**, Eli Lilly's diabetes drug, which booked **$8.7 billion** in a single quarter, edging out Keytruda's $7.9 billion. A drug that didn't exist five years ago now sits at the very top of the global pharmaceutical league table.

That single data point tells you everything about where the industry's center of gravity has moved. The GLP-1 class - the family of drugs behind Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound, has become the most valuable franchise in medicine. And in 2026, the war for it entered a new and far more interesting phase: **the pill.**

For commercial teams, this is the storyline of the year. The injectable era was about manufacturing capacity and shortages. The pill era is about reach, price, and who can pull the next wave of patients into the market. Here's how it is actually unfolding.

---

## The scoreboard

The Q1 2026 earnings season laid the rivalry bare. Two companies, the same drug class, and two completely opposite directions of travel.

**Eli Lilly** is in a class of its own right now:

- Total revenue of **$19.8 billion**, up **56%** year over year
- **Mounjaro** (diabetes): **$8.7B**, up 125% - now the world's #1 drug
- **Zepbound** (obesity): **$4.2B**, up 80%
- Combined, the two tirzepatide brands delivered nearly **$13 billion in a single quarter**
- Strong enough that Lilly *raised* its full-year 2026 guidance to **$82-85 billion**

On prescription share, Lilly's dominance is even starker: Mounjaro now captures more than 55% of new diabetes incretin prescriptions, and Zepbound holds close to 70% of new branded obesity prescriptions in the US.

**Novo Nordisk**, the company that *created* this market, is playing defense:

- Injectable Wegovy grew **12%** year over year (to roughly $2.8B equivalent), solid, but a slowdown from the growth of prior years
- Ozempic, its diabetes drug, **declined 8%**
- The broader obesity-care franchise still grew 22% at constant currency
- But the headline that spooked investors: Novo guided to a full-year **decline of 5% to 13%** in both sales and profit for 2026

> **Dashboard idea:** A side-by-side "growth divergence" chart - Lilly's +56% revenue line against Novo's projected decline, is the single most powerful visual you can lead with. It captures the entire narrative in one frame.

Why is the originator shrinking while the challenger soars? Three reasons: tirzepatide simply delivers more weight loss in head-to-head data, Lilly's patents run into the late 2030s while Novo faces semaglutide exclusivity loss in markets like China, Brazil, and Canada, and US price cuts are biting Novo harder. But the most important reason is strategic, and it's all about the pill.

---

## Why the pill changes everything

Until this year, every dollar in this market came from a weekly injection. That created two problems for the industry: needles scare off a large share of potential patients, and injectables are expensive and complex to manufacture at scale.

A daily oral pill solves both. And the commercial prize is enormous. Analysts at Goldman Sachs estimate oral GLP-1s could capture roughly **24% of the global weight-loss market - about $22 billion, by 2030**, in a category that could be worth close to **$100 billion annually** by the end of the decade.

Crucially, this isn't a fight over a fixed pie. Roughly **two-thirds of early pill volume is coming from patients entirely new to GLP-1 therapy.** Lilly's CEO David Ricks expects the global GLP-1 patient population to jump from about 20 million at the end of 2025 to **30 million by the end of 2026.** The pill isn't cannibalizing injections — it's growing the entire market.

That's the line commercial readers should underline: **the oral wave is expansion, not substitution.**

---

## Launch watch: Wegovy pill vs. Foundayo

Here's where 2026 gets genuinely competitive.

**Novo struck first.** It launched the **Wegovy pill** - the first oral GLP-1 for weight loss, in early January, and the rollout has been extraordinary. The drug hit around 50,000 weekly prescriptions within three weeks (about 45,000 of them self-pay), crossed **200,000 weekly prescriptions by mid-April**, and surpassed **2 million total prescriptions since launch**. Analysts called it one of the best pharmaceutical launches ever, and for a company badly in need of a win, it was a lifeline.

**Lilly answered in April** with **Foundayo (orforglipron).** Early prescription numbers have trailed the Wegovy pill's comparable launch window, but there's important context: Foundayo is an entirely new brand and molecule, so it has to build awareness from scratch. Ricks has told investors the ramp will take *"quarters, not days."*

The clinical and manufacturing nuances matter commercially:

- **Efficacy:** The Wegovy pill delivered roughly 17% weight loss over 64 weeks in its trial — strong, but still below the ~20% seen with Lilly's tirzepatide injectables.
- **Manufacturing:** Foundayo is a small-molecule pill rather than a peptide, which makes it dramatically **cheaper and simpler to produce at massive scale** — a structural advantage as both companies race to supply tens of millions of patients globally.
- **Price:** The Wegovy pill launched at cash prices of **$149 to $299 per month** depending on dose — already cheaper than the injections, and a signal that this market is moving toward affordability and the direct-to-consumer channel.

> **Dashboard idea:** A launch-curve comparison - weekly prescriptions for the Wegovy pill vs. Foundayo, indexed to "weeks since launch" - would let readers see the head start and the catch-up potential at a glance.

## The wildcard: pricing and policy

No commercial analysis of this market is complete without the policy overhang. Under the new **"Most Favoured Nations" (MFN)** pricing agreement, Novo's semaglutide medicines are getting broader, cheaper access through Medicare Part D, Medicaid, and self-pay channels. A **Medicare pilot covering obesity medicines is set to begin on July 1, 2026** — a potentially massive expansion of insured demand.

This is the double-edged sword of 2026: lower realized prices per prescription, but a far larger covered patient base. For Novo, the price cuts are a near-term drag on revenue. For the market overall, broader coverage could be the single biggest volume catalyst of the decade. How each company balances **price erosion against volume expansion** will define the winners.

---

## What to watch next

The obesity war is no longer a two-horse race confined to the US. Combination therapies like Novo's CagriSema promise a new tier of efficacy. Chinese players — Innovent, Sciwind, Hengrui - are launching domestically and eyeing global ambitions. And Pfizer, AstraZeneca, and others are buying their way in through licensing deals.

But for now, the scoreboard is clear. Lilly holds the efficacy edge, the manufacturing edge, and the patent runway. Novo holds first-mover advantage in oral and a launch that bought it credibility and time. Truist analysts project that Lilly's oral and injectable franchise combined could eventually reach **$101 billion in peak annual sales.**

The injectable era made GLP-1s the most valuable drugs on earth. The pill era will decide who owns them.
